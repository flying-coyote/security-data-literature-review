# Snowball candidates (2026-11-07)

Candidates, not bibliography entries. This file is the "identification via other methods" arm for the phase 1 upgrade pass: a one-hop citation-graph walk from the review's peer-reviewed anchors, ranked against the thin claims in `methods/claim-evidence-matrix-2026-11.md`. Rows marked ADMITTED were verified at a publisher, proceedings, author-hosted or abstract page and now sit in `MASTER-BIBLIOGRAPHY.md` under "Citation-Graph Snowball Admissions (2026-11-07)", with matching rows in `RESEARCH-JOURNAL.md`. Every other row is unverified beyond Crossref existence and should be read that way.

## Method and funnel

- **Seeds.** 40 peer-reviewed entries from the bibliography (the non-standards Level-A entries plus the DOI-bearing peer-reviewed Level-B entries); 35 resolved to a DOI (Crossref title match where the entry carried none, checked by hand for Samza and Delta Lake, where the title search returned the wrong record). Unseeded for lack of a DOI: Analyzing and Comparing Lakehouse Storage Systems (LHBench, CIDR 2023); Hyperscan: A Fast Multi-pattern Regex Matcher for Modern CPUs; Xor Filters: Faster and Smaller Than Bloom and Cuckoo Filters; Yang et al. - True Attacks, Attack Attempts, or Benign Triggers? An Empirical Measurement of Network Alerts in a Security Operations Center; PBC: High-Ratio Compression for Machine-Generated Data.
- **Backward.** Crossref `/works/{doi}` reference lists, keeping references that carry a DOI.
- **Forward.** OpenCitations v2 `citations/doi:` lists.
- **Records.** 2,812 unique DOIs (558 reached backward, 2237 forward, some both), of which 2776 returned OpenAlex metadata (title, venue, year, type, abstract) through 57 batched keyed calls. Full record set with per-record seed and direction: `methods/snowball-2026-11.json`.
- **Dedupe.** 15 records already in the bibliography and 16 already in the July PRISMA records (DOI or normalised-title match).
- **Triage.** A keyword score per thin claim plus a security-vocabulary bonus put 109 records at a score of 10 or more. The score is a sorting aid and it is noisy: forward citations of Sommer & Paxson and Axelsson are dominated by machine-learning intrusion-detection papers, many in low-integrity or predatory-looking venues, which were dropped by hand rather than scored down. The ranked list below is a human read of the top of that sort plus targeted lookups.
- **Targeted lookups.** Seven rows were identified by known-item search against a named thin claim rather than by the walk; they are labelled "targeted" because the walk could not reach them (their natural seeds, Yang et al. USENIX Security 2024 and the Arrow Flight SQL docs, carry no DOI).
- **Not done.** No agy/Gemini scouting; no Semantic Scholar graph walk (its unauthenticated limit stalled the abstract lookups at ~1 request every few seconds); one hop only.

## What the held rows need

- Julisch (TISSEC 2003) is the strongest available anchor for ch07's dead-tail rule triage, because its root-cause finding is the peer-reviewed form of "most alerts come from a few persistent causes"; it needs the abstract read at the ACM page or an author copy before admission.
- Wang & Strong (JMIS 1996) would replace DAMA-DMBOK (Tier B) as the source for ch03's data-quality dimensions; its abstract was read only through the OpenAlex copy, so admit after a primary read.
- Bhatt et al. (IEEE Security & Privacy magazine) and Oliner et al. (CACM review article) are refereed magazine pieces, so the tier is a ruling: §3.1 says peer-reviewed research, and a refereed review article is arguably that, but neither reports new measurement.

## Yield

Of 2,776 records with metadata, roughly 25 survived a human relevance read for a thin claim, 9 were verified and admitted (6 from the walk, 3 targeted), and 4 are held pending a primary read. The walk's yield on the security side is low because the security seeds are old and heavily cited by ML-IDS work; its yield on the data side (VLDB/SIGMOD neighbours of FastLanes, F3 and Delta Lake) is higher and cleaner.
